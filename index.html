01 — Approach
Four commitments that survive every platform I've built.
Tools change. These don't. They're the reason a platform is still trusted eighteen months after handover, and the reason an AI layer on top of it can be taken seriously.
01
Governance is architecture
Catalogued assets, least-privilege access, managed identities instead of stored secrets, audit trails that exist because of how the system is built rather than in spite of it. Retrofitting governance is a migration, not a setting, so it goes in on day one.
Unified catalog RBAC Zero-trust
02
Declarative over imperative
Describe what the data should be, not the sequence of steps to nudge it there. Medallion layers with real expectations between them, incremental loads with watermarks, idempotency by default, so lineage is a byproduct of the definition and re-running is boring.
Medallion Expectations Idempotency
03
Data engineering for AI
An agent is only as good as the data model beneath it. I structure and govern the layer agents retrieve from, favouring contextual retrieval over curated, permissioned context rather than raw dumps, and enforce anti-hallucination contracts so every column and join resolves against the schema. An agent that can't say "I don't have that" isn't an analyst, it's a rumour engine.
Contextual retrieval Anti-hallucination Governed context
04
Then I translate
Reporting and visualisation exist to make a decision easier to reach, not to display effort. I build the views that put the choice in front of people, take the analysis into the room in the language of the people funding it, and leave with a call that's been made rather than a dashboard that's been shown.
Sprint delivery Executive framing Decision support
